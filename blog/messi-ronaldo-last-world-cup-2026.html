Two Very Different Stories

Messi arrives in North America as a world champion. The burden that followed him through much of his international career — the question of whether he could ever match Maradona's 1986 triumph — has been lifted. Qatar 2022 was the perfect story: shaky start, brilliant recovery, sublime final. Everything from 2026 onwards is a bonus chapter in an already perfect story.

Ronaldo's journey carries a different emotional weight. He has conquered England, Spain, Italy and Europe with Portugal. He became the world's all-time leading international goalscorer. But the World Cup — the one trophy that has consistently eluded him — remains beyond reach. At 41, this is his final opportunity.

"As I've said before, I don't think I'll play another World Cup. At my age, it's only logical to think I might not." — Lionel Messi, September 2025

Both have favourable groups. Argentina should advance comfortably from Group J. Portugal face DR Congo, Uzbekistan and Colombia — a very winnable group. The tantalising possibility exists of Messi vs Ronaldo in a quarter-final on July 11 — potentially the last time these two players ever face each other.

A Record That Will Stand Forever

Both players are making history simply by being here. No player in history has ever appeared in six men's World Cups. They are doing it simultaneously. The record will almost certainly never be broken — the gap between them and the next generation is too large, and football's physical demands are too brutal for most players to remain at the elite level deep into their late thirties.

What Football Is About to Lose

When Messi and Ronaldo eventually walk away, football will lose more than records and trophies. It will lose a standard of excellence that forced everyone around them to improve. For over 15 years, the sport was defined by the question of which one was better. The debate is no longer relevant. What matters now is appreciating what is about to end.

There will be new stars — Lamine Yamal, Jude Bellingham, Florian Wirtz are all here in 2026. But there may never again be two players who dominate the sport simultaneously for so long, at such a level, while carrying the weight of global expectation every single week.

The Farewell Generation

Messi and Ronaldo are not the only legends playing their final World Cup. Luka Modrić (Croatia), Mohamed Salah (Egypt) and Son Heung-min (South Korea) are all likely playing their last tournament. Every match they play will be watched with the knowledge that it might be the last time we see them at this level.

The 2026 World Cup will be remembered for many things — the expanded format, the North American backdrop, the new rules. But the story that will bleed through every broadcast, every pre-match montage, every close-up at full time, is older and simpler than any of that. It is the story of goodbye.
