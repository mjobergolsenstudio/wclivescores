Messi: History Rewritten in Two Games

Before a ball was kicked, serious questions surrounded Messi. At 38, playing his club football in MLS, heading into a tournament with a fitness concern — reasonable people wondered if this World Cup might be a farewell lap rather than a final chapter.

Two games in, those questions look foolish. A hat-trick against Algeria — his first ever at a World Cup — was followed by a brace against Austria. Five goals in two games, including a moment that put him alongside and then beyond Miroslav Klose as the highest scorer in World Cup history with 18 goals.

The hat-trick against Algeria on June 16 landed on the exact 20-year anniversary of the day Messi scored his first World Cup goal as a teenager against Serbia and Montenegro. Even by his standards, it was a remarkable piece of timing.

Haaland: Norway's Golden Generation Arrives

The question was never whether Haaland would score. He scored 16 goals in European qualifying — twice as many as any other player — as Norway completed a perfect qualifying campaign. The question was whether this Norway side was good enough to go deep into the tournament and give Haaland the games he needed.

Two goals against Iraq, two more against Senegal. Four goals in two games, then rested for the France match. Haaland is already Norway's all-time leading World Cup scorer after two appearances. The knockout stage begins for Norway against Ivory Coast on June 30 — if they advance, Haaland's involvement in this race will intensify quickly.

Can Anyone Break Fontaine's Record?

Just Fontaine scored 13 goals in one World Cup — in 1958, in six matches. No one has reached double figures in the 68 years since. The 2026 format is the first in history where a team reaching the final plays eight matches. For the first time, the mathematics makes double figures achievable for a prolific striker on a deep run.

At the current scoring rate, Messi needs to average less than a goal per game to finish with 13. So does Haaland. The race has barely started and the record is already in serious conversation.

Our Prediction

Messi — because Argentina look capable of reaching the final, he takes penalties, and at 38 he knows this is his last chance. The record will mean more to him than it will to Haaland, who has time. Class is permanent, and Messi is proving it one more time.
