World Cup 2026 New Rules Explained: VAR for Corners, Red Cards for Covering Your Mouth and More

Published June 4, 2026 · 6 min read

The International Football Association Board (IFAB) has ratified the biggest package of rule changes in years — and the 2026 World Cup will be the first major tournament to use them. From expanded VAR powers to red cards for players who cover their mouths, here's everything that's changing on June 11.

FIFA's chief refereeing officer Pierluigi Collina confirmed: "These amendments aim to tackle discrimination, cut time-wasting, enhance match tempo and improve both the player and fan experience."

1. Expanded VAR Powers

📺
VAR can now review corner kicks and second yellow cards

Previously, VAR could only intervene for goals, penalties, direct red cards, and mistaken identity. Now VAR officials have authority to also review:

  • Incorrectly awarded corner kicks (if review can be completed without delaying restart)
  • Second yellow cards that were wrongly issued
  • Cases where the wrong player was penalised
  • Offences committed before the ball is in play at set-pieces

Note: VAR can correct a wrongly awarded second yellow, but cannot add a second yellow that wasn't given.

✓ Big positive for fairness

2. Red Card for Covering Your Mouth

🟥
Players will be sent off for covering mouth during confrontations

Any player who covers their mouth with their hand, arm or shirt during a confrontational situation will now receive a straight red card. The rule targets players who whisper inappropriate comments to teammates or opponents while hiding their lips from cameras.

The same applies to team staff who instruct players to leave the pitch, and any player who leaves the pitch in protest over a decision.

⚠ Watch for early red cards

3. Anti-Time-Wasting: Countdowns and Corner Kicks

⏱
Countdowns for goal kicks and throw-ins — corner for delays

Countdowns are introduced for goal kicks, throw-ins and substitutions. If a goal kick is delayed beyond the countdown, the opposing team will be awarded a corner kick.

For substitutions: players must now leave the field within 10 seconds of the board being shown and must exit via the nearest boundary line. If they fail to do so, their replacement cannot enter until the next stoppage — and only after one minute has passed.

✓ Will speed up the game significantly

4. Medical Treatment — One Minute Off the Pitch

🏥
Outfield players must leave pitch for one minute after treatment

Any outfield player who receives medical attention on the pitch must now remain off the field for at least one minute after play restarts. This targets the tactical use of "injuries" to waste time or allow teammates to regroup.

Exceptions apply for goalkeeper injuries, head injuries, suspected concussions, serious collisions, and players designated to take a penalty kick.

⚖ Controversial — one minute may be too long

5. Hydration Breaks — Every Half

💧
Three-minute water break in each half

Every match at the 2026 World Cup will feature a three-minute hydration break in each half, taken around the 22nd minute at the referee's discretion. Given the summer heat in the USA, Mexico and Canada, this is a necessary adjustment for player welfare.

✓ Sensible for player safety

What This Means for the Tournament

The combined effect of these changes should produce faster, more dynamic matches with less cynical play. The countdown timers alone could add several minutes of actual football per game — and with 104 matches being played, that adds up to a lot of extra football across the tournament.

Teams that rely heavily on time-wasting tactics — slow substitutions, players "injured" at convenient moments, goalkeepers dragging their feet — will need to adapt immediately.

The VAR expansion is the most significant change for match outcomes. A controversial second yellow card in a knockout match could now be overturned, which may prove decisive in the later stages of the tournament.

🔑 Quick Summary — New Rules at a Glance

  • VAR can review wrongly awarded corners and second yellow cards
  • Red card for covering mouth in confrontational situations
  • Goal kick delays → corner kick awarded to opponents
  • 10 seconds to leave pitch after substitution board shown
  • Outfield players off for 1 min after on-pitch treatment
  • 3-minute hydration break in each half (~22nd minute)
