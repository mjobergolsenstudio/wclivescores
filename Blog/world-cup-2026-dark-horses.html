World Cup 2026 Dark Horses: 7 Underdogs Who Could Shock the World

Published May 20, 2026  ·  9 min read

Every World Cup produces its underdog story. In 2002, South Korea reached the semi-finals. In 2018, Croatia made the final. In 2022, Morocco became Africa's first ever semi-finalist. With the expanded 48-team format in 2026, underdogs have more room than ever to make history. Here are the seven teams most likely to do it.

📖 Underdog History at the World Cup

2002 South Korea reached the semi-finals — beating Spain, Italy and Germany
2018 Croatia reached the final — beating Argentina, Russia and England
2022 Morocco became the first African semi-finalist — beating Belgium, Spain and Portugal

Japan — The Tactical Masterclass

GROUP F · ODDS: 40/1
40/1 to win the tournament

Japan are the most dangerous underdog in the 2026 World Cup — and the statistics back it up. In 2022, they beat both Germany and Spain in the group stage, two of the tournament's heaviest favourites. Their squad is even better now.

What makes Japan uniquely dangerous is their collective style. Unlike teams that rely on one star player, Japan function as a complete unit. Their pressing intensity, tactical discipline and intelligent movement off the ball create problems that even elite teams struggle to solve.

Why they can go deep
  • Beat Germany AND Spain in 2022 — this team knows how to beat favourites
  • No reliance on one star — entire squad performs as a unit
  • Mitoma, Kubo and Ito are among the most exciting attackers in Europe
  • Group F (Netherlands, Sweden, Tunisia) is very winnable
  • Tactical flexibility — can switch systems mid-game
Group: F
Opener: Jun 14 vs Netherlands
Coach: Hajime Moriyasu
#2
🇲🇦

Morocco — No Longer a Surprise

GROUP C · THE MOST COMPLETE AFRICAN TEAM

Morocco are no longer a dark horse in the traditional sense — they are a genuine contender. Their 2022 semi-final run transformed them from surprise package into a team that no longer fears elite opponents. That confidence is the most dangerous thing about them.

Hakimi, Ziyech and En-Nesyri lead a squad with quality across every position. Their defensive organisation is world-class — they conceded just one goal (an own goal) in five knockout matches in 2022.

Why they can go further
  • Semi-finalists in 2022 — tournament experience at the highest level
  • Defensive record: one goal conceded in 5 knockout matches at Qatar 2022
  • Hakimi (PSG) and Ziyech (Chelsea) — world-class individual quality
  • Group C with Haiti and Scotland — should qualify comfortably
  • No fear factor against big teams — they beat Spain and Portugal in 2022
Group: C
Opener: Jun 12 vs Brazil
2022: Semi-final
#3
🇳🇴

Norway — The Haaland Factor

GROUP I · ODDS: 33/1
33/1 to win the tournament

Norway haven't been at a World Cup since 1998. But they return with arguably the most dangerous striker on the planet. Erling Haaland set a UEFA qualifying record with 16 goals — a number that says everything about his current form and hunger.

The 48-team format is perfect for Norway. They need to win two matches and avoid a heavy defeat to France to advance. Against Iraq and Senegal, that's very achievable. And once Haaland is in a knockout game, anything can happen.

Why they can shock everyone
  • Haaland — the world's best striker in his prime World Cup
  • Ødegaard — one of the best playmakers in the Premier League
  • 16 qualifying goals for Haaland — a UEFA record
  • 28-year absence creates massive motivation and national energy
  • Iraq is a beatable first match — a big win boosts confidence
Group: I
Opener: Jun 15 vs Iraq
Last WC: 1998
#4
🇺🇾

Uruguay — Bielsa's Pressing Machine

GROUP H · QUIETLY DANGEROUS

Uruguay under Marcelo Bielsa have become one of the most tactically sophisticated teams in South America. Their high-pressing system surprised at Copa América 2024 and travels well to the physical demands of a World Cup.

Darwin Núñez leads the attack with explosive pace and power. Valverde is one of the best box-to-box midfielders in the world. Uruguay's Group H — with Spain, Saudi Arabia and Cape Verde — is tough, but a second-place finish is very achievable.

Why they can surprise
  • Bielsa's pressing system is among the most well-drilled in world football
  • Darwin Núñez — explosive, powerful, unpredictable
  • Valverde (Real Madrid) — one of the best midfielders in the world
  • Uruguay have a rich World Cup history — 2 titles, consistent dark horse
  • Can qualify 2nd in Group H behind Spain
Group: H
Opener: Jun 15 vs Saudi Arabia
Key player: Darwin Núñez
#5
🇺🇸

USA — The Host Nation Advantage

GROUP D · ODDS: 20/1
20/1 to win the tournament

Host nations historically overperform by roughly one round beyond their seeding. The USA have Pulisic (Chelsea), Reyna, McKennie and a young squad with genuine quality — but it's the home crowd factor that makes them genuinely dangerous.

Playing at MetLife Stadium, SoFi Stadium and other massive US venues in front of passionate home crowds could propel the USA further than any previous generation. Group D with Paraguay, Australia and Türkiye is very winnable.

Why they can go deep
  • Host nation advantage — historically worth one extra round
  • Pulisic in form at Chelsea — genuine Premier League quality
  • Young, hungry squad with nothing to lose
  • Group D is very winnable — Paraguay, Australia, Türkiye
  • Enormous home crowd energy at every match
Group: D
Opener: Jun 12 vs Paraguay
Key player: Pulisic
#6
🇸🇳

Senegal — Africa's Best Balance

GROUP I · ODDS: 40/1
40/1 to win the tournament

Senegal are described by analysts as Africa's best balance of attack and defence after Morocco. Sadio Mané leads a squad with quality throughout — Idrissa Gueye, Ismaïla Sarr and Edouard Mendy give them strength in every line.

In Group I with France, Iraq and Norway, Senegal are the second favourite to qualify behind France. If they get out of the group, a deep knockout run is genuinely possible.

Why they can surprise
  • Mané — still world-class, motivated for one final World Cup run
  • Africa's 2nd strongest team after Morocco
  • Defensive solidity — Mendy (Chelsea) one of the best GKs in the world
  • Experience: 2022 group stage eliminators, 2002 quarter-finalists
  • Group I — realistically second behind France
Group: I
Opener: Jun 15 vs France
Key player: Mané
#7
🇹🇷

Türkiye — The Quiet Danger

GROUP D · FLIES UNDER THE RADAR

Türkiye often flies under the radar — and that's exactly what makes them dangerous. Hakan Çalhanoğlu (Inter Milan) is one of the best midfielders in Serie A. Arda Güler and Baris Yildiz bring young, exciting talent, while Demiral provides defensive leadership.

Türkiye have quietly built a balanced squad blending young European-based talent with experienced players. Group D with USA, Paraguay and Australia is very competitive — but Türkiye can qualify.

Why they can surprise
  • Çalhanoğlu — one of the best midfielders in Europe, peak form at Inter
  • Güler (Real Madrid) — 20 years old, one of the most exciting young players alive
  • Flies under the radar — no pressure, nothing to lose
  • Tactically disciplined, hard to break down
  • 2002 World Cup semi-finalists — they know how to do it
Group: D
Opener: Jun 13 vs Australia
Key player: Çalhanoğlu
